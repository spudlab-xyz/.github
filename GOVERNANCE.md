# Governance

## Current model

Spudlab is currently founded and maintained by one person. The founder sets priorities, maintains the organisation's repositories and makes final decisions, including what can be supported safely with the time available.

This is a practical description of the project's present state, not a claim of institutional authority or a permanent structure.

## Decisions and proposals

Significant technical, scientific or community changes should normally begin with a public issue or proposal in the relevant repository. The discussion should identify the problem, evidence, alternatives, trade-offs and compatibility implications. Final reasoning should be recorded in the issue, pull request or related documentation.

Routine maintenance and reversible changes may proceed through an ordinary pull request. Security, privacy or conduct matters may need private handling.

## Disagreement and concerns

Constructive disagreement is welcome. Address the substance of a proposal, support claims with evidence where possible and state uncertainty. Raise technical or governance concerns in the relevant issue or in a new issue if the original discussion is unsuitable. Conduct concerns must use the private process in the Code of Conduct once that process is active; they should not be disclosed publicly.

The founder makes the final decision when consensus is not reached, explaining the reasoning publicly where privacy and security allow.

## Future maintainers

As participation grows, contributors may become maintainers after demonstrating sustained, constructive work, sound judgement, reliable review and care for the project's users and scientific principles. Responsibilities and repository access should be agreed explicitly, introduced gradually and documented publicly. There is no automatic entitlement based on contribution count or tenure.

## Practical limits

Spudlab is volunteer-maintained. Work may pause, priorities may change and not every proposal can be accepted or supported. Maintainers should communicate limits honestly, avoid commitments they cannot sustain and favour a manageable scope over unsupported promises.
